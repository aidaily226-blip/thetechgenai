Amazon Operates More Than 1 Million Robots in Its Warehouses

Something unusual is happening inside Amazon warehouses around the world.

Instead of employees moving every product manually, robots are increasingly transporting inventory, moving containers, and helping sort packages. Some machines travel across warehouse floors carrying heavy loads, while others use robotic arms to handle products.

Amazon operates more than 1 million robots across its operations network. The company has been steadily increasing its use of warehouse automation, investing in artificial intelligence and robotics to make its fulfillment operations faster and more efficient.

But what does this actually mean? When did Amazon start using robots? Which countries are using them? And are these machines replacing human warehouse workers?

Here is the full story.

---

## Who Is Amazon?

Before anything else — for international readers who may not know the company.

Amazon is one of the world's largest e-commerce and technology companies. It operates online shopping platforms, fulfillment centers, delivery networks, cloud computing services, and other businesses.

Its warehouses, also called fulfillment centers, store products before they are packed and shipped to customers.

Amazon has been investing in robotics for more than a decade. In 2012, it acquired Kiva Systems, a robotics company that developed mobile machines capable of moving shelves of products to warehouse employees.

That acquisition helped Amazon begin a major shift toward automated fulfillment operations.

Today, Amazon uses different types of robots for transporting inventory, moving packages, and supporting warehouse employees. The company continues developing more advanced machines that can navigate independently and handle a wider variety of tasks.

This is not simply an experiment from a robotics startup. Amazon is using automation across a large logistics network and continuing to develop new robotic systems.

---

## When Did Amazon Start Using Warehouse Robots?

Amazon's modern warehouse robotics journey began in 2012, when it acquired Kiva Systems.

The company's early mobile robots were designed to move shelves containing products to employees, reducing the amount of walking required inside fulfillment centers.

Over the following years, Amazon developed and introduced additional robotic systems for different warehouse activities.

In 2025, Amazon announced that it had deployed its one millionth robot. The milestone demonstrated how significantly the company had expanded its use of robotics since its early experiments.

Amazon continues to develop newer systems that can move carts, transport containers, sort packages, and help employees handle inventory.

## Which Countries Are Using Amazon Warehouse Robots?

Amazon's robotics operations are international, but the company does not publish a complete, regularly updated list of every country and warehouse using each robot model.

Some confirmed examples include:

### United States

The United States is a major center for Amazon's robotics operations.

Amazon has deployed mobile robots and robotic systems in its American fulfillment network. Its facilities use automation to move inventory, transport packages, and support warehouse operations.

The company also develops and tests new robotic technologies in the United States.

### Japan

Japan is another confirmed location in Amazon's robotics network.

In 2025, Amazon delivered its one millionth robot to a fulfillment center in Japan. This marked an important milestone in the company's global deployment of warehouse automation.

### Spain

Amazon introduced its STARK robotic tote-handling system through a pilot in Barcelona, Spain.

The company announced plans to expand STARK to additional European sites, demonstrating its intention to increase automation across its European fulfillment operations.

### United Kingdom

Amazon has showcased robotics technologies at facilities in the United Kingdom, including its Dartford fulfillment center.

The company has also announced plans for newer robotic systems to reach Europe, including an upgraded Proteus robot expected to arrive in the region in 2027.

These examples show that Amazon's warehouse robotics activity extends beyond the United States. However, they should not be treated as a complete list of countries where every Amazon robot is currently operating.

## Why Is Amazon Increasing the Use of Robots?

Amazon's growing use of robots is driven by several operational goals.

### Faster Order Processing

Robots can transport products and inventory across large fulfillment centers, helping reduce the time employees spend walking between storage areas.

### Handling Heavy Inventory

Some Amazon robots move heavy inventory pods and containers that would otherwise require substantial manual effort.

### Improving Warehouse Efficiency

Artificial intelligence can help coordinate robot movements, plan routes, and reduce unnecessary travel inside warehouses.

### Supporting Growing Delivery Demand

Amazon processes large volumes of online orders. Automation can help the company manage inventory and package movement as its fulfillment network grows.

---

## Are Amazon Robots Replacing Human Warehouse Workers?

This is one of the biggest questions surrounding Amazon's robotics expansion.

Robots are increasingly handling repetitive physical tasks such as transporting inventory, moving containers, and sorting packages. As automation expands, Amazon may need fewer workers for certain activities or may avoid hiring as many employees as it would have without robots.

However, robots do not perform every warehouse task.

Employees continue to handle work such as resolving inventory problems, maintaining equipment, supervising operations, and managing situations that require judgment or flexibility.

The impact of automation is therefore more complicated than simply saying that Amazon is replacing every human worker with a robot.

Robotics can change the number of employees required for certain tasks, the types of jobs available, and the skills workers need.

---

## What Happens Next?

Amazon is continuing to develop more advanced warehouse robots.

Newer systems are designed to navigate more independently, understand instructions, move containers, and handle products with greater precision.

The company is also expanding its European robotics plans and introducing additional automation technologies into its fulfillment network.

As these systems improve, Amazon is likely to automate more warehouse activities over time.

The exact pace of expansion will depend on the cost of robotics, reliability, operational requirements, and how effectively machines can work alongside employees.

---

## Conclusion

Amazon operates more than 1 million robots across its global operations network and continues steadily increasing its use of warehouse automation.

Its robotics activity is confirmed in locations including the United States, Japan, Spain, and the United Kingdom, although a complete country-by-country deployment list is not publicly available.

Amazon's long-term direction is clear: more AI-powered machines, greater automation of repetitive warehouse tasks, and increasingly integrated human-robot operations.

The key question is not simply whether robots will replace people. It is how warehouse jobs, productivity, and the skills needed to work in modern logistics will change as automation expands.
