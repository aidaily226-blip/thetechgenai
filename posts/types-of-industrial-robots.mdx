# Types of Industrial Robots: 6 Main Robot Types, Uses and How They Differ

Industrial robots are used in factories to automate tasks such as welding, assembly, material handling, painting, packaging, inspection and palletizing.

But industrial robots are not all built the same way. Their mechanical structure, number of axes, reach and movement determine what they are best suited for.

The main types of industrial robots include **articulated robots, Cartesian robots, SCARA robots, Delta robots, cylindrical robots and spherical robots**.

Collaborative robots, commonly called **cobots**, are another important category in modern manufacturing. Unlike the categories above, cobots are primarily defined by how robots are designed to work with people rather than by one particular mechanical structure.

## What Is an Industrial Robot?

An industrial robot is a programmable machine designed to perform physical tasks automatically in manufacturing and other industrial environments.

Industrial robots can be programmed to repeatedly move parts, weld components, assemble products, pick objects from conveyors, apply paint, inspect products or place finished goods onto pallets.

The important difference between industrial robot types is **how the robot moves and how its mechanical structure is designed**.

## The 6 Main Types of Industrial Robots

| Robot type | Typical movement | Common applications |
|---|---|---|
| Articulated | Rotary joints | Welding, assembly, material handling |
| Cartesian | Linear X-Y-Z movement | Machine handling, assembly, 3D printing |
| SCARA | Horizontal rotary movement | Electronics assembly, pick and place |
| Delta | Parallel-arm movement | High-speed picking and packaging |
| Cylindrical | Rotary and linear movement | Machine loading, material handling |
| Spherical/Polar | Rotary and linear movement | Handling and specialized applications |

The International Federation of Robotics includes articulated, Cartesian/linear/gantry, cylindrical, spherical/polar, parallel and SCARA configurations among industrial robot types.

# 1. Articulated Robots

Articulated robots are among the most recognizable industrial robots.

They use a series of rotary joints that allow the robot arm to move through a large working area. Industrial articulated robots commonly have four, five or six axes, although other configurations are also possible.

A six-axis articulated robot can move its tool in multiple directions and orientations, making it useful for complex manufacturing operations.

### How Articulated Robots Work

The robot has connected arm sections joined by rotating joints. Motors at the joints control the movement of each section.

The robot controller coordinates these movements to position the end of the arm at a programmed location.

Depending on the application, the end effector could be:

- A welding torch
- A gripper
- A paint applicator
- A drilling tool
- A polishing tool
- A material-handling device

### Common Applications

Articulated robots are commonly used for:

- Automotive welding
- Machine tending
- Assembly
- Painting
- Material handling
- Palletizing
- Inspection
- Packaging

### Advantages

The biggest advantage of an articulated robot is **flexibility**.

It can reach around objects, approach a workpiece from different angles and perform tasks requiring complex movements.

### Limitation

Articulated robots can require more programming, safety equipment and installation space than some simpler robot designs.

---

# 2. Cartesian Robots

Cartesian robots are also called **linear robots or gantry robots**.

Instead of relying mainly on rotating joints, they move along linear axes. A typical Cartesian robot uses three perpendicular axes corresponding to the X, Y and Z directions.

### How Cartesian Robots Work

Imagine a machine moving:

- Left and right
- Forward and backward
- Up and down

That is the basic principle behind Cartesian movement.

Because the robot operates within a coordinate system, positioning can be straightforward for applications that fit within a defined working area.

### Common Applications

Cartesian robots are used for:

- Machine loading and unloading
- Pick-and-place operations
- Assembly
- Packaging
- Material handling
- Automated machining systems
- 3D printing
- Precision positioning

### Advantages

Cartesian robots can provide:

- Precise linear movement
- Good repeatability
- Coordinate-based programming
- Efficient use of overhead space in gantry configurations

### Limitation

Their rectangular working area can make them less suitable for applications that require the robot to reach around complex objects.

---

# 3. SCARA Robots

**SCARA** stands for **Selective Compliance Assembly Robot Arm**.

SCARA robots are designed particularly well for fast assembly and pick-and-place operations.

They typically use horizontal rotary joints combined with vertical movement, giving them fast and precise motion for many assembly tasks.

### How SCARA Robots Work

A typical SCARA robot has a compact arm that moves horizontally while another axis provides vertical movement.

This gives the robot a combination of:

- Fast horizontal movement
- Vertical positioning
- Controlled rotation

This configuration makes SCARA robots useful when products need to be picked, positioned and assembled quickly.

### Common Applications

SCARA robots are commonly used for:

- Electronics assembly
- Small-part assembly
- Pick and place
- Packaging
- Screwdriving
- Component insertion
- Inspection

### Advantages

Speed is one of the main advantages of SCARA robots.

For repetitive assembly tasks where the robot does not need the full range of movement offered by a six-axis articulated robot, a SCARA system can be an efficient solution.

### Limitation

SCARA robots are not designed for every type of three-dimensional manipulation. Their movement is particularly suited to applications involving horizontal positioning and vertical operations.

---

# 4. Delta Robots

Delta robots are recognizable by their lightweight parallel-arm design.

Instead of using a conventional robotic arm with joints arranged one after another, Delta robots use multiple arms connected to a moving platform.

### Why Delta Robots Are Fast

Much of the robot's heavier machinery can remain above the working area.

The moving parts can therefore be relatively light, allowing rapid movement.

This makes Delta robots particularly useful when a factory needs to pick and place many products in a short period.

### Common Applications

Delta robots are often used for:

- Food packaging
- Pharmaceutical packaging
- Sorting
- Pick and place
- Small product handling
- Conveyor-based production

For example, a Delta robot can identify products moving along a conveyor and rapidly move selected items into different containers.

### Advantages

- Very high speed
- Good repeatability
- Efficient for pick-and-place operations
- Suitable for high-volume production

### Limitation

Delta robots are generally less versatile than articulated robots for tasks requiring complex orientations or work around large objects.

---

# 5. Cylindrical Robots

Cylindrical robots use a combination of rotary and linear movement that produces a cylindrical working area.

They are one of the established configurations used in industrial robotics.

### How Cylindrical Robots Work

A typical cylindrical robot can rotate around a central axis while its arm moves vertically or horizontally.

The resulting movement allows the robot to work within a roughly cylindrical workspace.

### Common Applications

Cylindrical robots have been used for:

- Machine loading
- Material handling
- Assembly
- Welding
- Component handling
- Manufacturing operations around a central workstation

### Advantages

Their mechanical design can work well where components are arranged around a central area.

### Limitation

Cylindrical robots are less common in many modern applications than articulated, SCARA and Cartesian systems.

---

# 6. Spherical or Polar Robots

Spherical robots, sometimes called **polar robots**, use rotary and linear joints to create a spherical or partially spherical working envelope.

### How Spherical Robots Work

Their joints allow the arm to rotate and extend, giving the robot a curved working area around its base.

This design provides a different type of reach compared with Cartesian robots, which primarily operate along straight linear axes.

### Applications

Spherical robots have historically been used for applications such as:

- Material handling
- Welding
- Die casting
- Machine operations
- Industrial handling tasks

### Are Spherical Robots Still Common?

They are less prominent in many modern factory applications than articulated, SCARA, Cartesian and Delta systems.

However, understanding the spherical configuration is useful when learning how industrial robot designs have evolved.

---

# What Are Collaborative Robots?

Collaborative robots, or **cobots**, are designed for applications where robots and people can work in closer proximity under appropriate safety conditions.

A cobot should not be considered simply another mechanical robot structure.

Instead, "collaborative robot" describes a robot and application designed for interaction with workers under appropriate safety requirements.

Cobots can use different mechanical configurations depending on their intended application.

### Common Cobot Applications

Cobots can be used for:

- Assembly
- Machine tending
- Quality inspection
- Packaging
- Screwdriving
- Material handling
- Palletizing

The required safety assessment depends on the robot, application, tooling, workspace and applicable standards.

A robot should not be assumed to be safe for direct human contact simply because it is marketed as a cobot.

---

# Industrial Robot Types vs. Robot Applications

One common source of confusion is the difference between a **robot type** and a **robot application**.

For example:

**Articulated** describes the robot's mechanical structure.

**Welding** describes what the robot is doing.

Therefore, an articulated robot can be used for welding, painting or material handling.

Similarly, a SCARA robot can be used for assembly or pick and place.

This distinction is important when choosing an industrial robot because the best robot depends on both its movement capabilities and the production task.

# Which Type of Industrial Robot Is Best?

There is no single industrial robot that is best for every factory.

The correct choice depends on factors such as:

- Required speed
- Payload
- Reach
- Accuracy
- Number of axes
- Workspace
- Product size
- Production volume
- Required flexibility
- End effector
- Safety requirements
- Integration with existing equipment

A simple way to compare them is:

| If you need... | Consider... |
|---|---|
| Complex multi-directional movement | Articulated robot |
| Straight X-Y-Z movement | Cartesian robot |
| Fast assembly | SCARA robot |
| High-speed pick and place | Delta robot |
| Work around a cylindrical area | Cylindrical robot |
| Spherical or curved working range | Spherical or polar robot |
| Robot-worker collaboration | Collaborative robot, depending on the application |

The final choice should be based on the complete production process rather than the robot's name alone.

# How AI Is Changing Industrial Robots

Industrial robots have traditionally relied heavily on programmed motions and predefined production processes.

Modern robotics is increasingly incorporating technologies such as machine vision, artificial intelligence, predictive maintenance and more autonomous decision-making.

AI can help robots process information, detect patterns, improve planning and support more flexible automation.

For example, machine vision can allow a robot to identify the position of objects on a conveyor rather than relying only on fixed positions.

AI can also be used alongside traditional robot programming rather than replacing it completely.

This is an important distinction:

**A robot's mechanical type and its intelligence are two different things.**

An articulated robot can use conventional programming, machine vision, AI-based software or a combination of technologies.

# Industrial Robot Types and Their Applications

Different industries choose different robot configurations because their production requirements are not the same.

### Automotive Manufacturing

Automotive factories commonly use articulated robots for applications such as welding, painting, assembly and material handling.

### Electronics Manufacturing

Electronics production can benefit from SCARA robots because of their speed and precision in small-part assembly.

### Food and Packaging

Delta robots are particularly useful for fast picking, sorting and packaging applications.

### Warehousing and Material Handling

Different robot configurations can be used for machine tending, picking, palletizing and moving products.

### General Manufacturing

Cartesian, articulated and collaborative robots can be used across many manufacturing environments depending on the required reach, payload, speed and flexibility.

# How to Choose an Industrial Robot

Choosing an industrial robot should start with the production task rather than the robot brand.

Consider these questions:

### 1. What does the robot need to move?

Determine the size and weight of the product or tool.

### 2. How far must it reach?

The robot needs enough reach to access every required position in the workspace.

### 3. How fast does it need to work?

High-volume production may require a robot designed for rapid repetitive movements.

### 4. How precise does it need to be?

Assembly and precision manufacturing may require high repeatability and accurate positioning.

### 5. Does the robot need multiple orientations?

If the tool must approach a component from many different angles, an articulated robot may be more suitable.

### 6. Will people work near the robot?

If humans and robots need to share a workspace, the complete application should be evaluated for appropriate collaborative safety measures.

# The Future of Industrial Robotics

Industrial robotics is moving beyond simple repetitive automation.

Developments in AI, machine vision, sensors, simulation and robot software are helping manufacturers create systems that can adapt to more varied tasks.

The International Federation of Robotics has identified **AI and autonomy** as major robotics trends, alongside developments in humanoid robots, energy efficiency and robotics security.

This could make industrial automation more flexible, particularly in factories where products and production requirements change frequently.

However, AI does not eliminate the importance of mechanical design.

The physical structure of the robot still determines how it can move, how much it can carry and where it can operate.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## What are the 6 main types of industrial robots?

The six major industrial robot configurations commonly discussed are articulated, Cartesian, SCARA, Delta/parallel, cylindrical and spherical/polar robots.

## What is the most common type of industrial robot?

Articulated robots are one of the most widely used industrial robot configurations because their multiple joints provide flexibility for applications such as welding, assembly, painting and material handling.

## Which industrial robot is best for welding?

Articulated robots are commonly used for welding because their multiple rotary joints allow welding tools to approach components from different angles.

## Which industrial robot is best for pick and place?

Delta robots are particularly suitable for high-speed pick-and-place applications. SCARA and Cartesian robots can also be effective depending on the product and workspace.

## What is a SCARA robot used for?

SCARA robots are commonly used for assembly, pick and place, component insertion, packaging and other repetitive operations requiring fast horizontal movement.

## What is the difference between Cartesian and articulated robots?

Cartesian robots move primarily along linear X, Y and Z axes, while articulated robots use rotary joints. Cartesian robots are well suited to defined linear workspaces, while articulated robots provide greater flexibility of movement.

## What is a Delta robot?

A Delta robot is a parallel robot that uses multiple lightweight arms connected to a moving platform. Its design allows very fast movement, making it useful for pick-and-place and packaging applications.

## What is a collaborative robot?

A collaborative robot, or cobot, is designed for applications where robots and people can work in closer proximity under appropriate safety requirements. Cobot describes the collaborative application rather than one specific mechanical structure.

## Can industrial robots use artificial intelligence?

Yes. Industrial robots can use AI with technologies such as machine vision, perception, planning and predictive systems. AI can complement traditional robot programming and make automation more adaptable.

## How much does an industrial robot cost?

The cost varies considerably depending on the robot type, payload, reach, accuracy, controller, end effector, software and installation requirements. The purchase price of the robot is only one part of the total automation cost.

# Final Takeaway

The main **types of industrial robots** are distinguished by their mechanical structure and movement.

**Articulated robots** provide flexibility, **Cartesian robots** provide precise linear movement, **SCARA robots** are well suited to fast assembly, and **Delta robots** excel at high-speed picking and packaging. **Cylindrical and spherical robots** use different movement geometries for more specialized applications.

There is no universal "best" industrial robot. The right choice depends on the task, payload, reach, speed, accuracy, workspace and safety requirements.

As AI and machine vision become more capable, industrial robots are also becoming more flexible. But the fundamental mechanical design remains important: **how a robot moves determines what it can do.**
