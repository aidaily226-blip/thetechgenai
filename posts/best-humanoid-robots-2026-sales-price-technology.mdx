Top 9 Best Humanoid Robots in 2026: Sales, Price and Technology Compared

Humanoid robots are moving beyond laboratory demonstrations in 2026. Companies are developing robots that can walk, pick up objects, move materials, work in factories and perform repetitive tasks in warehouses.

However, the most famous robot is not necessarily the one selling the most units. Some companies are already shipping thousands of robots, while others are concentrating on advanced artificial intelligence, industrial pilot projects or future home applications.

According to Counterpoint Research, global humanoid robot shipments exceeded 22,000 units in the first half of 2026, rising nearly 300% year over year. The research firm expects worldwide shipments to exceed 50,000 units during 2026.

This article compares nine leading humanoid robot platforms based on reported shipments, technology, commercial progress, practical uses and available pricing.

**Important:** Sales and shipment figures are not always directly comparable. Research firms use different definitions, and some companies do not publish detailed sales numbers. This list distinguishes reported shipment leaders from other major robots that are technologically important but lack comparable public sales figures.

## 1. AgiBot: The Leading Humanoid Robot by Reported Shipments

**Country:** China  
**Robot families:** A-series, X-series and G-series  
**Best suited for:** Research, services, entertainment and industrial applications

### Sales performance

AgiBot ranks first in Counterpoint Research's first-half 2026 shipment report, with approximately 9,700 robots shipped and more than 43% of the tracked market.

The company has developed multiple robot families for different purposes rather than relying on a single humanoid model. Its growing shipment volume suggests that commercial availability and production capacity matter as much as advanced demonstrations.

AgiBot is also pursuing real-world deployments, including retail and service applications. These deployments help demonstrate how humanoids might operate outside research laboratories.

### Technology

AgiBot's portfolio includes:

- **A-series:** Full-size humanoids designed for human-facing and general-purpose applications.
- **X-series:** More compact humanoids for services, research and other applications.
- **G-series:** Robots focused on industrial and general-purpose embodied AI tasks.
- **Embodied AI:** Integrating perception, learning, movement and interaction so robots can respond to real environments.

Its different robot families allow the company to target applications ranging from demonstrations and retail to factory operations.

### Price

A single public price for the entire AgiBot range is not available. Industrial deployments may require a quotation based on the model, hardware, software and support.

**Verdict:** AgiBot is a leading choice to examine if reported shipment volume is your main ranking criterion.

Source: [AgiBot official website](https://www.agibot.com/)

## 2. Unitree G1: An Accessible Humanoid for Robotics Development

**Country:** China  
**Robot:** Unitree G1  
**Best suited for:** Research, education, robotics development and experimentation

### Sales performance

Unitree ranked second in Counterpoint Research's H1 2026 shipment data, with more than 7,000 humanoid robots shipped and approximately 31% of the tracked market.

Its G1 series is an important part of the company's humanoid portfolio. Compared with many industrial robots sold through enterprise contracts, the G1 has a published online price, making it easier for research organisations and developers to evaluate its cost.

### Technology

The Unitree G1 combines compact humanoid hardware with dynamic movement and manipulation capabilities.

Key features include:

- Multiple joint configurations, depending on the version.
- Dynamic balance and coordinated movement.
- Dexterous-hand options for object manipulation.
- Imitation learning and reinforcement-learning-based movement.
- A developer-focused EDU edition for programming and research.

The standard G1 and EDU edition are not interchangeable in every respect. Buyers should check Unitree's specifications to confirm which development features are included.

### Price

Unitree's official store lists the G1 from **$13,500**, excluding shipping, customs duties and applicable taxes. The EDU edition is listed separately.

The final landed price can be higher for international buyers, especially after shipping, import clearance and local taxes.

### What can it do?

The G1 can support research into walking, balance, manipulation, imitation learning and human-robot interaction. It is not automatically a ready-to-work replacement for a human employee: specific tasks may require programming, integration and safety testing.

**Verdict:** One of the most interesting options for developers and research organisations seeking a relatively accessible humanoid robot.

Source: [Unitree G1 official product page](https://shop.unitree.com/collections/humanoid-robot/products/unitree-g1)

## 3. Galbot G1 and S1: Humanoids for Industrial and Logistics Work

**Country:** China  
**Best suited for:** Industrial handling, logistics and specialised service operations

### Sales performance

Counterpoint Research reported that Galbot shipped more than 1,100 humanoid robots in H1 2026, representing approximately 5% of the tracked market.

The company is pursuing applications where robots must manipulate objects and perform tasks in real working environments.

### Technology

Galbot's work focuses on connecting visual perception and AI planning with physical actions.

Its capabilities and development priorities include:

- Recognising objects and their positions.
- Using vision-language-action approaches for real-world tasks.
- Manipulating items in industrial and logistics environments.
- Adapting to work settings through task-specific development.
- Building robots for specialised applications rather than entertainment alone.

The Galbot S1 uses a wheeled dual-arm configuration for industrial and logistics scenarios. It is not a conventional two-legged humanoid, so it is better understood as a related embodied-AI platform than a strict bipedal robot.

### Price

A confirmed public retail price for the relevant Galbot systems was not available. Industrial customers generally need to contact the company for a quotation.

**Verdict:** Particularly interesting for businesses that prioritise material handling and specialised operations over human-like walking.

## 4. UBTECH Walker S: Built for Industrial Manufacturing

**Country:** China  
**Robot:** Walker S series  
**Best suited for:** Automotive factories and electronics manufacturing

### Sales performance

UBTECH ranked among the leading suppliers in Counterpoint's first-half 2026 shipment report, with deliveries reaching approximately the thousand-unit level.

The company has also announced ambitious shipment targets for its Walker S series. A target, however, is not the same as a confirmed full-year sales result.

Walker S is designed to work in environments such as factory production lines. Its published capabilities include:

- Visual, audio and distance sensors.
- Three-dimensional perception and semantic navigation.
- Hand-eye coordination for object handling.
- Multimodal AI-based decision-making.
- Language-model integration for interpreting instructions and planning tasks.
- Teleoperation and robotics software capabilities.

These features help the robot perceive its surroundings, navigate and manipulate objects. Actual performance depends on the task, factory environment and system integration.

### Price

UBTECH does not provide a simple, universal public retail price for Walker S. Businesses generally need to request an enterprise quotation.

### Why it matters

Factories are among the most promising environments for humanoid robots because work is repetitive, measurable and often performed in structured spaces. Each task must still be tested for safety, reliability and economic value before large-scale deployment.

**Verdict:** A major contender for industrial automation, particularly in manufacturing environments.

## 6. Figure 03: Advanced Physical AI for Factory Work

**Country:** United States  
**Robot:** Figure 03  
**Best suited for:** Manufacturing, material handling and complex factory workflows

### Sales performance

Figure is an important example of why shipment rankings and technology rankings can differ.

The company has not provided a comparable public H1 2026 customer-sales figure in the sources used here. However, its industrial progress is significant, with Figure robots being developed and deployed for factory work.

Figure has previously reported that its earlier Figure 02 robots contributed to production tasks at BMW. That production milestone measures factory activity, not the number of robots sold.

### Technology

Figure's Helix system is designed to translate visual information into coordinated physical actions.

Its areas of focus include:

- Picking and placing parts with varying positions and orientations.
- Coordinating hands, arms, torso and feet.
- Repositioning the body while manipulating objects.
- Handling objects in changing environments.
- Adapting movements instead of following only a fixed sequence.

These capabilities are important because real factories are not perfectly predictable. Objects shift, bins become disorganised and robots must adjust to small changes.

### Price

Figure 03 does not have a confirmed public retail price. It is an enterprise-oriented system, and pricing is not directly comparable with an off-the-shelf research robot.

**Verdict:** One of the most interesting platforms for advanced factory automation, particularly when tasks require coordinated movement and manipulation.

Source: [Figure official website](https://www.figure.ai/)

## What Do Humanoid Robot Sales Tell Us About the Future?

The most important trend in 2026 is the difference between producing robots and proving that they can perform useful work economically.

Three developments deserve attention.

### 1. Chinese manufacturers are scaling production

AgiBot, Unitree, Galbot, UBTECH and Leju feature prominently in shipment reports. Their growing production volumes indicate that the humanoid market is no longer driven exclusively by prototypes and laboratory research.

### 2. Industrial customers want measurable productivity

Factories and warehouses are attractive early markets because tasks can be measured. A company can evaluate the number of objects moved, successful task completion, downtime, maintenance costs and the amount of human supervision required.

A robot that works reliably on a limited task may deliver more immediate value than a more advanced robot that struggles with everyday variations.

### 3. Home robots face a different challenge

A home contains pets, children, clutter, stairs, fragile objects and unpredictable human behaviour. A robot must handle this complexity while maintaining safety and privacy.

That is why a published home-robot price is only one part of the decision. Buyers also need evidence of useful capabilities, reliable service and real-world availability.

## Final Verdict: Which Are the Best Humanoid Robots in 2026?

**AgiBot leads this comparison by reported H1 2026 shipment volume**, while Unitree stands out for its combination of shipment scale and published prices for selected models.

UBTECH and Galbot are important to watch for industrial work. Figure 03 is notable for complex factory tasks, and Agility Digit has commercial commitments in warehouse and manufacturing logistics. Tesla Optimus remains a high-profile long-term contender, while 1X NEO represents the push toward home assistance.

The market is evolving quickly, so the most useful ranking is not simply the robot with the most impressive demonstration. It is the robot that can be delivered, perform the required task consistently, operate safely and justify its total cost.

For businesses considering humanoid robots in 2026, the practical question is no longer just *which robot looks the most advanced?* It is *which robot can reliably complete a useful job at an acceptable cost?*
