Pollen Robotics Microduck: 7 AI Features That Make You Want to Buy This Robot

Something interesting is happening in robotics. Pollen Robotics has introduced Microduck, a tiny AI-powered robot that can walk, grab objects, recover after falling, and learn new movements through reinforcement learning.

Standing approximately 25 cm tall and weighing under 800 grams, Microduck is designed to make physical AI more accessible. It combines a duck-like appearance with programmable movements, sensors, and open-source software.

But here is what makes it especially interesting: Microduck is not just a robot to watch. You can control it, play with it, experiment with its behaviours, and explore how AI can make a physical machine move.

Could it be a robotic pet, an educational tool, or a fun gadget for technology lovers? Let's explore seven features that make Pollen Robotics Microduck worth considering.

## What Is Pollen Robotics?

Pollen Robotics is a robotics company based in Bordeaux, France, that became part of Hugging Face in 2025. The company develops robots and software for human-robot interaction, research, education, and experimentation.

Microduck takes a different approach from a conventional voice assistant. Its primary focus is physical movement: learning how to walk, interact with objects, recover from falls, and perform new behaviours.

Its software ecosystem also gives developers an opportunity to experiment with reinforcement learning and train their own movement policies.

You can explore the [official Pollen Robotics website](https://pollen-robotics.com/).

## 7 AI Features That Make You Want to Buy Pollen Robotics Microduck

### 1. It Can Walk on Two Legs Using 15 Motors

One of Microduck's most impressive features is its ability to walk on two legs.

The robot uses 15 motors to control its articulated body, including its legs, head, and neck. Its walking behaviour is powered by a trained movement policy rather than simply rolling across the floor on wheels.

**How is this useful?**

- **For entertainment:** You can watch your little robotic companion waddle around.
- **For learning:** Students can explore balance, movement control, and bipedal robotics.
- **For developers:** It provides a real platform for experimenting with AI-controlled movement.

Two-legged walking is a challenging robotics problem, making Microduck an interesting way to explore how software controls a physical machine.

### 2. It Can Learn New Tricks Through Reinforcement Learning

Microduck supports reinforcement learning, a machine-learning technique that allows a system to learn behaviours through repeated attempts and feedback.

Developers can train movement policies in a physics simulator, test them, and transfer suitable policies to the physical robot.

The process generally involves training a behaviour, evaluating it in simulation, deploying it on the robot, and refining it when necessary.

**How is this useful?**

- **For AI learners:** You can explore how reinforcement learning works beyond theory.
- **For programmers:** You can experiment with training and modifying movement behaviours.
- **For robotics enthusiasts:** You can investigate how an AI policy behaves when transferred from simulation to real hardware.

The exciting part is that the robot's built-in movements are a starting point. With the appropriate software and technical skills, developers can experiment with additional behaviours.

However, teaching it a new trick is not necessarily as simple as giving it a spoken command. Training and testing may require programming and experimentation.

### 3. It Can Grab Objects With Its Beak

Unlike many robots that use conventional robotic hands, Microduck has an articulated beak designed to grasp suitable small objects.

It can lower its body, move its beak toward an object, scoop it up, and return to an upright position.

**How is this useful?**

- **For play:** You can experiment with small objects and simple physical games.
- **For education:** It demonstrates object manipulation and coordination between different body movements.
- **For developers:** It creates opportunities to explore grasping behaviours and movement policies.

Imagine using it to pick up a suitable lightweight object or incorporating object-grabbing into a robotics experiment.

It is not a heavy-duty robotic arm, though. Its size and gripping mechanism limit the objects it can handle.

### 4. It Can Get Back Up After Falling

Walking robots can lose their balance, and recovering from a fall is an important challenge in robotics.

Microduck includes a trained recovery behaviour that can help it return to a standing position after many common falls.

**How is this useful?**

- **For play:** You do not necessarily have to pick it up after every supported fall.
- **For learning:** You can observe how a robot handles movement failures.
- **For development:** You can explore recovery policies and the challenges of controlling a physical machine.

This feature is especially interesting because real-world robotics is not only about performing an action correctly. It is also about handling situations in which something goes wrong.

Recovery is not guaranteed from every position or on every surface.

### 5. It Can Sit, Stand, Kick, Turn, and Roller-Skate

Microduck comes with seven trained movements, including walking, turning, sitting and standing, kicking, driving, grabbing, and recovering from falls. Roller-skating movement is also available when the appropriate skates are equipped.

The included game controller lets you begin playing with the robot without having to write code first.

**How is this useful?**

- **For entertainment:** You can experiment with different movements and play simple games.
- **For families and educators:** It can make robotics demonstrations more engaging.
- **For developers:** You can study existing behaviours and use them as a starting point for new experiments.

You could imagine setting up a small obstacle course, experimenting with a ball, or comparing how the robot behaves in different movement scenarios.

The exact result depends on the environment, available accessories, and the robot's programmed behaviours.

### 6. It Has a Camera, LiDAR, and Motion Sensors

Microduck includes several sensors that help provide information about its surroundings and its own movement.

Its listed hardware includes a front-facing camera, a compact time-of-flight LiDAR sensor, and two inertial measurement units (IMUs). It also has microphones, a speaker, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth connectivity.

**How is this useful?**

- **For experimentation:** Developers can investigate how robots use visual and motion information.
- **For education:** Students can learn why robots need sensors to interact with the physical world.
- **For custom projects:** Sensor data can provide a foundation for experimenting with new behaviours.

The camera and sensors also make it an interesting platform for exploring future interactive robotics applications.

However, having sensors does not automatically make Microduck fully autonomous. Its capabilities depend on its software and configuration.

### 7. It Can Act Like a Small Robotic Pet

This is one of the most appealing aspects of Microduck for people who love unusual gadgets.

Its duck-like appearance, waddling movements, little sounds, and ability to perform playful actions can make it feel more like a small robotic creature than a conventional machine.

Pollen Robotics describes its audio as creature-like rather than spoken conversation. Each robot gets its own generated audio identity, but that does not mean it automatically understands human language or develops emotions.

**How is this useful?**

- **For companionship-style entertainment:** You can watch it move around and interact with it through its supported controls.
- **For play:** You can try movement-based games, object-grabbing activities, and other behaviours.
- **For customisation:** Developers can experiment with additional software behaviours to make interactions more interesting.
- **For technology lovers:** It offers a different kind of companion gadget that combines personality with programmable robotics.

Could it replace a dog or cat? No. It does not provide the natural affection or instincts of a living pet. It is better understood as a programmable robotic companion that can be fun to interact with.

Advanced abilities such as recognising its owner, following someone around reliably, or holding natural conversations should not be assumed to work out of the box.

## What Can Pollen Robotics Microduck Not Do?

Although Microduck has several interesting features, it is important to understand its limitations before buying it.

### It Is Not a Full Conversational AI Assistant

Microduck has microphones and a speaker, but its main purpose is physical movement and robotics experimentation.

It does not automatically function like ChatGPT or a smart speaker that can answer general questions and hold natural conversations. Additional software development would be required for more advanced interactions.

### It Cannot Replace a Real Pet

Its appearance and movements can make it feel pet-like, but it does not have the natural instincts, needs, or emotional experiences of a living animal.

It is a robotic companion for play and experimentation rather than a replacement for a dog, cat, or other real pet.

### It Cannot Perform Every Household Task

Microduck is not designed to clean rooms, wash dishes, cook meals, or carry heavy objects.

Its compact size and specialised design limit what it can do.

### It Requires Care and Technical Experimentation

The robot has a removable battery and a limited operating time. Its battery life is approximately one hour, depending on usage.

While its built-in behaviours work without writing code, training new behaviours and modifying its software may require programming and troubleshooting.

As physical AI develops, small robots like Microduck could help students, developers, and enthusiasts understand how intelligent systems move beyond computer screens.
