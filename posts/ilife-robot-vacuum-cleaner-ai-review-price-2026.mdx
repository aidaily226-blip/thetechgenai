## What is iLife? Who Makes It?

## Is iLife Robot Vacuum Actually AI Powered?

This is the question that genuinely matters. The answer is: **it depends on which model you buy.**

iLife makes a wide range of robot vacuums — from very basic entry-level models to more advanced AI-driven units. They are not all the same.

### Entry Level — No Real AI

The V-series models (like the V3s Pro and V5s Pro) use simple **random path cleaning**. The robot bounces around your room in a random pattern until it has covered the floor. There is no mapping, no route planning, and no AI involved. These are the cheapest models and the most basic in terms of technology.

### Mid Range — Basic Navigation

Mid-range models like the A10 use **infrared sensors** and basic navigation algorithms. They follow a more structured cleaning path than the random-bounce models, but still do not use real machine learning or AI mapping.

### High End — Genuine AI Features

<cite index="19-1">The iLife X1000 is described as a commercial and home AI robot vacuum — and it genuinely earns that label. It uses 3D Time-of-Flight sensors and LiDAR technology, has AI recognition built in, and supports Amazon Alexa and Google Home integration.</cite>

<cite index="21-1">The X1000 specs include 3D Time-of-Flight sensors combined with LiDAR navigation technology, AI recognition capability, a 200-minute runtime, and an extraordinary 20,000Pa suction — one of the highest in any consumer robot vacuum globally. It includes a 3-litre multi-inlet cyclonic separation dustbin and connects to the ILIFE Clean app on both iOS and Android.</cite>

The A20 Pro is another AI-capable model. <cite index="16-1">It has a 5200mAh battery, LiDAR laser navigation, and different cleaning modes — making it great for apartments and large homes. With 6500Pa of suction power and a dual-brush design, it handles tiles, wood, and low-pile carpets effectively.</cite>

## What Processor Does iLife Use?

iLife does not publicly disclose the specific processor chips used in their robots — which is common practice for most consumer robot vacuum brands. However based on the technology inside these devices, here is what is actually happening:

**Entry level models (V-series):** Use basic embedded microcontrollers handling simple sensor inputs and motor control. No dedicated AI chip.

**Mid range models:** Use ARM-based processors capable of handling Wi-Fi connectivity and app communication. Basic computational power.

**High end models (X1000, A20 Pro):** <cite index="17-1">Use LiDAR navigation technology for precise mapping and efficient cleaning routes.</cite> These models likely use a dedicated navigation processor combined with a main application processor — similar to how smartphones separate the main chip from the camera processor.

The AI recognition feature on the X1000 — which identifies obstacles and adjusts cleaning behaviour — requires a neural processing capability, suggesting a dedicated AI chip or AI-capable SoC (System on Chip) in the flagship models.

## Should You Buy an iLife Robot Vacuum in 2026?

The honest answer depends entirely on what you are buying.

**Buy the iLife V3s Pro or V5s Pro if:** You want the cheapest possible robot vacuum that actually works, you have a small apartment with mostly hard floors, and you do not care about smart mapping or app control. At $120-$165, nothing else comes close for basic functionality.

**Buy the iLife A20 Pro or T10s if:** You want real LiDAR navigation and app control at a mid-range price. These compete with significantly more expensive brands and represent genuinely good value.

**Buy the iLife X1000 if:** You need commercial-grade cleaning for a large space — a restaurant, office, or hotel lobby — and want AI obstacle recognition and 20,000Pa suction in one device.

**Skip iLife if:** You want the absolute best mapping accuracy, the most reliable obstacle avoidance, or a premium self-emptying system. For that, Roborock, Ecovacs, or the higher end of the Shark lineup will serve you better — at a higher price.
