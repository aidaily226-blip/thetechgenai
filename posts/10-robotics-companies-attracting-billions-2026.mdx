Agile Robots Raises $500 Million: 10 Robotics Companies Attracting Billions in 2026

Robotics is becoming one of the biggest investment opportunities in the technology industry. Companies developing humanoid robots, industrial automation systems, autonomous machines and AI-powered robot software are attracting billions of dollars from global investors.

One of the latest examples is **Agile Robots**, a German robotics company that raised $500 million in a funding round led by SoftBank in November 2026. The investment valued the company at approximately $8.5 billion.

But Agile Robots is not the only company attracting major investment. From AI-powered robot brains to humanoid machines designed for factories and warehouses, several companies are receiving substantial financial backing.

Here are 10 robotics companies attracting billions of dollars in funding, investment or financing commitments.

## 1. Agile Robots – $500 Million Funding Round

**Country:** Germany  
**Latest funding round:** $500 million  
**Reported valuation:** $8.5 billion

<https://www.agile-robots.com/>

Agile Robots is one of the major European companies developing intelligent industrial robotics systems. Founded in 2018 as a spin-off from the German Aerospace Center (DLR), the company focuses on connecting artificial intelligence with physical machines.

Its technology includes robotic arms, industrial automation systems, robotic hands, mobile robots and humanoid robots.

In November 2026, Agile Robots announced a $500 million funding round led by SoftBank. NVIDIA and Hitachi also participated in the investment.

The new funding is intended to support international expansion, particularly in the United States, and increase production capacity.

The company has reportedly raised around $2 billion in total funding since its foundation. This makes it an important example of how investors are backing companies that can bring AI into real industrial environments.

## 2. Skild AI – $1.4 Billion Funding Round

**Country:** United States  
**Latest funding round:** $1.4 billion  
**Reported valuation:** More than $14 billion

<https://www.skild.ai/>

Skild AI is developing a general-purpose AI brain for robots. Instead of building only one type of robot, the company wants to create software that can control different robotic systems and help them perform different tasks.

Its approach is based on what the company calls omni-bodied intelligence.

The company announced a $1.4 billion funding round in January 2026, led by SoftBank, with participation from NVIDIA's NVentures and other investors.

The investment pushed its reported valuation above $14 billion.

Skild AI is particularly interesting because its business focuses on the intelligence behind robots rather than only the mechanical hardware.

## 3. Atoms – $1.7 Billion in Tracked Funding

**Country:** United States  
**Tracked funding:** Approximately $1.7 billion

Atoms is listed among the largest-funded companies in the robotics sector in 2026.

Its investment profile reflects the growing interest in companies developing advanced robotic and AI systems.

The reported funding figure is a tracked company-level total, not necessarily one individual funding round. Investors are increasingly interested in companies that can combine AI software, hardware and autonomous capabilities.

As robotics expands beyond traditional factory automation, companies working on general-purpose physical AI are attracting more attention.

## 4. Figure AI – Approximately $1.7 Billion in Tracked Funding

**Country:** United States  
**Tracked funding:** Approximately $1.7 billion  
**Reported valuation:** $39 billion

<https://www.figure.ai/>

Figure AI is developing humanoid robots designed to work in environments built for humans.

Its robots are intended for tasks in manufacturing, warehousing, logistics and other industries where businesses need additional labour.

The company has attracted major investment from technology and financial investors. Its Series C funding included a reported $1 billion investment and valued the company at approximately $39 billion.

Figure is also working on AI systems that allow humanoid robots to understand instructions, learn tasks and interact with their surroundings.

Its long-term goal is to make humanoid robots useful in commercial workplaces and eventually expand their applications.

## 5. NEURA Robotics – Approximately $1.5 Billion in Tracked Funding and Commitments

**Country:** Germany  
**Tracked funding and financing:** Approximately $1.5 billion

<https://neura-robotics.com/>

NEURA Robotics is another German company attracting major attention in the robotics industry.

It develops cognitive robots, humanoid robots and collaborative robotic systems designed to work alongside people.

Unlike conventional industrial robots that mainly perform pre-programmed movements, cognitive robotics aims to make machines more capable of understanding their surroundings and responding to different situations.

NEURA has attracted substantial financing interest in 2026. However, its reported financing figure should not be interpreted as cash already received from a single completed funding round.

Its technology is focused on bringing intelligent robots into factories, workplaces and everyday environments.

## 6. Physical Intelligence – More Than $1 Billion in Tracked Funding

**Country:** United States  
**Tracked funding:** Approximately $1.1 billion

<https://www.physicalintelligence.company/>

Physical Intelligence is working on general-purpose AI models for robots.

The idea is to develop AI that can help different robotic systems perform tasks without requiring completely separate software for every machine.

This is an important challenge in robotics. A robot may be able to perform one task very well but struggle when it encounters a different object, environment or instruction.

Physical Intelligence wants to make robots more adaptable by developing AI models that can understand and perform a wider range of physical tasks.

Its funding reflects investor interest in the software and intelligence layer of the robotics industry.

## 7. Mind Robotics – Around $1 Billion in Tracked Funding

**Country:** United States  
**Tracked funding:** Approximately $1 billion

Mind Robotics is developing AI-powered robotics technology with a focus on industrial applications.

The company is associated with the growing movement toward physical AI, where artificial intelligence moves beyond computer screens and interacts with the physical world.

Its investment profile demonstrates that investors are interested not only in companies producing humanoid robots but also in the software and systems that make industrial machines more autonomous.

The tracked funding figure represents reported company-level funding, rather than a single $1 billion round.

## 8. Waymo – $16 Billion Funding Round

**Country:** United States  
**Reported funding round:** $16 billion

<https://waymo.com/>

Waymo is known for its autonomous driving technology and robotaxi services.

Although it is not a humanoid robot manufacturer, its self-driving vehicles are examples of advanced autonomous robotic systems operating in the real world.

Waymo combines artificial intelligence, cameras, sensors, mapping and computing systems to help vehicles navigate roads and respond to traffic conditions.

Its reported $16 billion funding round in 2026 highlights the enormous investment potential of autonomous systems.

The company's technology also demonstrates how robotics can be applied beyond factories, warehouses and industrial environments.

## 9. Zipline – Approximately $2.1 Billion in Tracked Funding

**Country:** United States  
**Tracked funding:** Approximately $2.1 billion

<https://www.flyzipline.com/>

Zipline develops autonomous drone delivery systems.

Its technology is designed to transport medical supplies, healthcare products and other goods through automated aerial delivery networks.

Instead of relying entirely on conventional delivery vehicles, Zipline uses autonomous aircraft and software to coordinate deliveries.

The company has expanded its operations across multiple markets and has become an important example of commercial robotics being used outside traditional industrial settings.

Its tracked funding figure is a company-level total, rather than a single $2.1 billion round.

## 10. Re:Build Manufacturing – Approximately $2.1 Billion in Tracked Funding

**Country:** United States  
**Tracked funding:** Approximately $2.1 billion

<https://www.rebuildmanufacturing.com/>

Re:Build Manufacturing focuses on advanced manufacturing and industrial technology.

It is not exclusively a robotics company. However, its activities are connected to the broader industrial automation ecosystem, where advanced manufacturing, engineering and robotics can work together.

Companies in this area are important because large-scale robotics adoption requires more than robot development. It also needs manufacturing facilities, engineering capabilities, production systems and industrial infrastructure.

Its tracked funding places it among the major investment names in the broader robotics and mobility landscape.

## Why Are Investors Putting Billions Into Robotics?

Several factors are contributing to the growth of robotics investment.

### 1. Labour shortages

Factories, warehouses and logistics companies need workers for repetitive, physically demanding and sometimes dangerous tasks. Robots could help businesses address some of these challenges.

### 2. AI is making robots more capable

Traditional robots generally perform tasks based on programming and predefined instructions. New AI models are helping robots understand instructions, process visual information and respond to changing environments.

### 3. Humanoid robots are attracting attention

Companies are developing robots with human-like bodies because many workplaces are already designed around human movement, tools and equipment.

### 4. Industrial automation is expanding

Manufacturing companies are investing in robotic arms, autonomous mobile robots, inspection systems and automated production lines to improve efficiency.

### 5. Investors are backing the entire robotics ecosystem

The opportunity is not limited to companies manufacturing robots. Investors are also supporting companies developing AI models, sensors, chips, control systems, autonomous navigation and robotic infrastructure.

## Conclusion

The robotics industry is entering a major investment phase. Agile Robots' $500 million funding round is one example of how established industrial robotics companies are attracting large investors.

At the same time, companies such as Skild AI, Figure AI, NEURA Robotics and Physical Intelligence are working on the intelligence that could make future robots more capable.

The biggest opportunity may not belong to only one humanoid robot manufacturer. It could be shared across the entire ecosystem: hardware manufacturers, AI software developers, autonomous systems companies and industrial automation providers.

As technology improves and production costs change, the next few years will show which companies can turn large investments into commercially successful robotics products.
